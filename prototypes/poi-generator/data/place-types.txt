# Place types: what a kind of place has, each thing's real size range in metres, and how the things relate.
# No positions and no layouts: the planner (src/Gen/Arch/PlaceTypes.cs) rolls every spawn's own sketch from
# these, from its seed and its world's size, so no two spawns of a type are the same place.
#
#   type <id>
#     name     what it is called
#     tech     the tech levels it is built at
#     share    the most of its world's area it may take (the planner shortens the long parts toward
#              their lower ends to fit, and says so when even those don't)
#     part <id> <kind> [what] [sizes] [relation]...
#       kinds     surface <cover>      levelled made ground, laid like the world's roads (tarmac, concrete, gravel, dirt)
#                 building <program>   a building of data/programs.txt, sized here, with its rooms planned at build
#                 tanks | lights | fence | road <cover>
#       sizes     len w depth h r count cab shoulder concourse: a..b is rolled by each spawn
#       relations along <part> gap a..b      parallel to it, its centre line gap metres off, either side
#                 between <a> <b>            across the ground from one to the other (links)
#                 beside <part> [away <q>]   off a long side of it (the side away from q), touching or gap a..b off
#                 ends <part>                in line off each end of it
#                 faces <part>               its front (doors, cab) turned to it
#                 near <part> a..b, far <part> a..           how far from it (edge to edge)
#                 sees <part>                nothing taller stands between it and that part
#                 from <part>                a road from it out to the place's gate
#                 round a..b                 round everything, that far out
#
# Scaled to our worlds (owner, 14 Nov: "airports don't have to be realistic size, they can be scaled down to our
# world. 250 m runways are fine", then "a 500m runway is fine"): the lengths between the parts keep a real airport's proportions (ICAO Annex 14 and
# FAA AC 150/5300-13 for runway, taxiway offset, apron and approach lighting), scaled to a 400 to 600 m runway; what a
# body or a craft uses stays true size. The biggest craft an airfield takes is a Small-class flight vessel, about 30 m
# across: runways 30 m wide, taxiways 15, aprons twice its span deep, hangar doors over 30 m wide; rooms, doors, ceilings
# and stairs at a person's size.

type airport
  name Airport
  tech mid high
  share 0.05
  part runway   surface tarmac   len 400..600 w 30 shoulder 5
  part taxiway  surface tarmac   w 15 along runway gap 60..80
  part link     surface tarmac   w 15 count 2..4 between runway taxiway
  part apron    surface concrete len 200..320 depth 70..100 beside taxiway away runway
  part terminal building terminal len 60..100 depth 30..40 h 10..14 concourse 6 beside apron away taxiway faces apron
  part tower    building control_tower len 8..10 depth 8..10 h 18..26 cab 5..7 beside apron away taxiway near terminal 10..100 sees runway
  part hangar   building hangar count 1..3 len 40..48 depth 30..40 h 16..20 beside apron away taxiway faces apron gap 0..6
  part fuel     tanks count 2..3 r 3..5 h 5..8 beside taxiway away runway far terminal 100..
  part carpark  surface tarmac   len 50..90 depth 30..50 beside terminal away apron gap 6..12
  part access   road tarmac      w 7..8 from carpark
  part lights   lights           len 100..200 ends runway
  part fence    fence            h 2..3 round 20..40

# Spaceports by starport class, from the same planner, scaled to our worlds and our ships the same way: the game's own
# ships land and lift off vertically, so a port is a row of pads (two to three times the craft across), each in its
# blast apron with a clearance to the next, a taxiway along them with a link out from each, an apron, halls the ships go
# into through doors they fit, a terminal, a control tower that sees the pads, the fuel and oxidiser stores out beyond
# the pads and apart along them (as Kennedy LC-39A keeps its two at opposite corners of its pad; NFPA 55 separates bulk
# oxidiser from fuel), a car park, an access road and a perimeter. No strip: a type names one only where its craft need
# it. The world's class picks the biggest it may be, its size the biggest that fits. Craft: a landing field takes Small-
# class vessels (about 30 m), a spaceport mid Large-class ones (about 60 m), a starport the biggest (about 100 m).
